Breaking the fusion ceiling: space then time

Person-coverage recall as each axis is added. Spatial fusion alone plateaus; the time axis breaks through.

Spatial overlap alone caps near 90% — even 9 cameras reach only 89.5%, because cameras that miss a hard case (smoke, hood, arm occlusion) tend to miss it together (correlated misses). Adding the time axis — where misses are far less correlated — lifts recall to 95.0%, near the 97.9% ceiling. Mechanism ladder with idealized cross-camera linking (§5-7); the realized world-coordinate deployment reaches 94.0% (§5-9).

Why predict? The stopping-distance budget (ISO/TS 15066 SSM)

To stop before contact, the robot must see and predict people out to a full prediction-reach disc — not just the stop ring.

Prediction turns a reaction into a head start. A person walking straight in covers 1.6 m/s × 1.6 s ≈ 2.6 m during the horizon, so anyone within ˜5.7 m can reach the stop ring before the robot could otherwise react — that is the disc the cameras must cover and the predictor must watch. Because the dominant term is detection + tracking latency, faster perception directly shrinks the required safety distance (project thesis, §5-12). Ring-to-metre scale (mPerAU) still needs field calibration (±25%).
